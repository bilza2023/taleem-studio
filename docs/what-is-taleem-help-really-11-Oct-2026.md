
# What is Taleem.help, really?

*Out-of-Beta review — findings and decisions. 11 October 2026.*

---

## The crux

**Concepts are already free. Numericals are where students get stuck, and nobody has built a library for them, which is why tuition exists.**

Taleem.help is a **Study-App for one board's syllabus (FBISE, Curriculum 2024)** in which **every textbook question has its own narrated, animated lesson** that keeps improving, **and every student question gets answered**. Good answers stay under that question for every future student.

It takes over the part of tuition that free platforms never touched: explaining *this particular question*, and answering doubts about it.

---

## 1. What Taleem.help is

- **A Study-App, not a video site.** Built on one government course, plus allied services, so the student lives in a learning environment (no gossip, no politics, not a Facebook).
- **Students use it like a key-book**, and that is fine. "One question, one presentation" is key-book indexing: stuck on Q7, open Q7. Many students will skip concept intros; that is the entry door, not a failure.
- **A lecture is an event; a presentation is an asset.** A live teacher's quality resets every day (new mistakes, fatigue, rush). Taleem's quality compounds, because every fix is permanent.

---

## 2. Launch plan: on-campus first

- **Phase 1:** local ads and admitted students at the Bahria Town Phase 8 campus. The first few batches also get **2 months of on-campus classes**.
- **The main product is online.** It opens to all of Pakistan gradually, after one or two courses have run on campus.
- **Campus months are onboarding, not tuition.** They teach students to use the app, build parent trust, seed the first Q&A, and give direct feedback on lessons.
- **Student limit = room size.** No self-signup; **admin creates accounts** for admitted students. No server-based capacity logic.
- **Content readiness rule:** every chapter must be complete before the batch reaches it.
- **Samples group:** good presentations are copied into a public "Samples" group, so courses can be finished at our own pace. (Copies drift from originals when originals are improved; re-copy periodically.)

---

## 3. Pricing

- **Rs 5,000 per subject per year.** Annual only, no monthly plans. Extendable for another year at a nominal fee.
- **Why annual:** the same app is used for every test and exam in that class, and students will buy other subjects (Physics etc.) later.
- **Benchmarks:**
  - WiseTutorHub (live online FBISE tuition): **Rs 7,000/subject/month** in groups of 5–10, **Rs 10,000/subject/month** one-to-one.
  - Cheapest local tuition assumed at ~Rs 5,000/subject/month. **Not verified online**; local rates aren't published. Verify by asking 4–5 Bahria Town academies.
- **The argument holds even if the assumption is off.** Taleem costs roughly 1/6 to 1/16 of one subject's yearly tuition.
- **Open:** are the 2 campus months included in the Rs 5,000, or a separate fee?

## 4. Market findings (online FBISE education)

### The field

| Type | Examples | What they really are |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube and its wrappers | Individual teachers, Sabaq.pk | Free; Sabaq's videos are hosted on YouTube |
| Live online tuition | WiseTutorHub | Paid tutor hours, mostly for overseas (GCC) families |
| Funded multi-board apps | Maqsad, Edkasa | Pivoted away from matric recorded content (see below) |
| Small sites | downloadclassnotes.com, campus.pk | Broken or neglected (raw shortcodes on video pages) |
| The board itself | FBISE Official App | Store listing claims video lectures; installed app has none |

### Sabaq.pk: the real benchmark, examined

- Self-funded non-profit since 2012. ~17–18k free videos KG–14, all boards, ~5M users. Also sells/sets up offline Chromebooks for schools (Blended Learning). **Its job is serving schools with weak teachers, not a student stuck at home.**
- **FBISE Class 9 Math, Chapter 1 (Real Numbers): 98 videos.**
  - The chapter list **is mapped to the new Curriculum 2024 book**. (Correction to an earlier assumption: Sabaq is *not* on the old syllabus at chapter level.)
  - But the videos are **older generic series re-tagged** into the new structure (IDs like `m-9-10-…`, one from the Grade 6–8 series; sections 1.1 and 1.2 point to the same video).
  - **The unit is the topic, not the textbook question:** ~45 concept videos plus ~53 of Sabaq's own example problems. **No video is titled for a textbook question.**
  - Textbook exercise solutions exist only as a **text PDF** (no narration, no video).
  - Views fall from ~28,700 on the intro video to a few hundred deeper in. (Caution: video IDs are shared across boards, so these numbers are probably summed across boards.)
- **Taleem Chapter 1: 66 questions, each its own presentation.**

### The core finding

- **Sabaq turned concepts into videos, not numericals.** That gap is why tutors and tuition exist.
- **Nobody we found has done question-by-question lessons systematically for a whole book.** On YouTube, exercise videos are typically one long video per exercise; you need timestamps to reach a sub-question.
- **Why nobody does it: their business models punish depth.**
  - Ad and SEO sites earn per page view, so breadth pays.
  - Funded startups need a big market, so they cover ~22 boards.
  - YouTube rewards long videos (watch time, mid-roll ads), not 60 short ones.
  - Live teaching produces one long recording; cutting it into questions is unpaid work.
  - Evidence: **Edkasa** moved toward live classes and "Connected Classrooms" for schools; **Maqsad** moved toward MDCAT/ECAT/BCAT entry-test prep.
- **The opening is structural, not temporary.** Their incentives won't change, so they won't change course.
- **The moat is time plus accumulated answers.** A copier would need ~1,500 reviewed, narrated presentations per book **and** a year or more of answered questions.

### Our own observations

1. **No seriousness:** most players are cash grabs (government included).
2. **No specialisation:** everyone does everything, bulk content, "PDF lay lo" mentality. "We only do this" is rare.
3. **No feedback loop:** sales pages, not community pages. Sabaq depends on free platforms, so it gets only coarse, borrowed data and can't act on drop-offs.

## 5. Our advantages

| Unlike them | Taleem |
|---|---|
| Multi-board, shallow, bulk content | **One board, one book, every question.** |
| Unread generated content | **Each deck is narrated and voiced by its author**, so it is checked many times before going live. Flood gates ≠ AI PDF. |
| Topic-level videos | **Question-level lessons**, matching the actual homework |
| Old videos re-tagged into the new book | **Made for Curriculum 2024**, and revised rather than re-recorded |
| Static once published | **Keeps improving**; every fix is permanent |
| Q&A on WhatsApp/Facebook, if anywhere | **Questions live under the lesson they're about**, on our own platform |
| Coarse platform analytics | **Per-question (even per-slide) usage data**, because the player is ours |
| Linking to individual videos forbidden (Sabaq) | **Every question has its own link**; sharing is encouraged |

## 6. The teacher is AI, with three ground realities

**The course teacher (answering student questions) is AI**, grounded in that question's own slides and narration, so its answer uses the same method and notation the student just watched.

**What this changes:** the time bottleneck disappears, answers are fast and always available, and the price works even with few students.

**Ground realities:**

1. **A wrong answer, once pinned, spreads.** Rule: **AI answers immediately; the author reviews before anything becomes an FAQ.** Same "checked many times" discipline as decks.
2. **Be honest with parents.** "AI" stays out of the marketing, but the product must never *claim* a human writes each reply. True phrasing: *questions are answered quickly and every published answer is checked by the teacher.*
3. **Free AI photo-solvers are the real competitor for answers.** What they can't give: answers matching the lesson, reviewed answers, accumulated FAQs per question, and narrated presentations on the current book. **AI is the engine; grounding, review and the archive are the value.**

**Open decision (when building Q&A):** AI answers appear instantly (marked unreviewed until checked), or wait for review?

## 7. The feedback loop: our strong point

The feedback loop fills in for the missing teacher.

| What a teacher does | In Taleem |
|---|---|
| Answers "I'm stuck here" | Ask under the question |
| Answers quickly | AI, grounded in the lesson; response time is the phase-1 number to watch |
| Doesn't repeat itself | Reviewed answers pinned as FAQs on that question |
| Notices where the class struggles | Usage data per question: views, rewatches, drop-offs |
| Improves the lesson | Revision queue driven by questions and usage |
| Tells the class what changed | "Updated" marks on lessons |

**The loop:** questions + usage → revision → better lesson → fewer questions.
It must live on our own platform (questions stay attached to lessons, the data is ours, no gossip). Facebook/YouTube only for casual chat.

---

## 8. Locked principles

1. **The question is the unit.** Every question is findable and linkable.
2. **Nothing goes live unreviewed** (decks and pinned answers alike).
3. **One board, one book, every question, on the current curriculum.**
4. **The feedback loop is the strong point** and lives on our own platform.
5. **The hub is a personal dashboard plus course notices, never a feed or chat.**

## 11. Home page review (from 10,000 feet)

- **Current state:** says who we are clearly, then asks "which class?" twice (hero buttons and class cards both go to `/course?course=…`; footer class links go to `/courses`), and never says how to join. Fees and FAQ are commented out. Samples section still has placeholder slugs.
- **Hero:** replace the three class buttons with two actions: **Watch sample lessons** and **Admissions**.
- **Positioning tension:** "Pakistan's Online Academy" and "Online, anywhere in Pakistan" vs an on-campus launch. Recommended: keep the tagline (brand), change the offer line to admissions at the Bahria Town Phase 8 campus.
- **Class cards:** in phase 1 they lead to half-built course pages; consider hiding them or pointing them to Admissions.
- **Admissions page** (not a "sale page"): classes offered, campus address and timings, limited seats per batch, WhatsApp/phone.
- **Polish:** remove "(Beta)"; fix mismatched fonts (Section headings render in serif); "Indepth" → "In-depth"; footer class links → `/course?course=…`; consistent use of `config.basePath`; remove "Create account" and close `/signup`.
